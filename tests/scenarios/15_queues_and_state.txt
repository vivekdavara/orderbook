# Time priority seen in the queues themselves. The state checks show each side's orders in priority order
# (id:price x remaining), the best prices, how many orders rest and the last accepted id (DESIGN.md, "The text
# format"); depth alone can't show the order of a queue.

# Three bids at one price queue by arrival; a better price goes ahead of them.
> new 1 buy limit 5 @ 100
accepted 1
> new 2 buy limit 6 @ 100
accepted 2
> new 3 buy limit 7 @ 100
accepted 3
> new 4 buy limit 1 @ 101
accepted 4
= bids 101x1 100x18
= orders bids 4:101x1 1:100x5 2:100x6 3:100x7
= best bids 101
= best asks none
= resting 4

# A sell takes order 4, then part of order 1, which keeps its place at the front with what is left.
> new 5 sell ioc 3 @ 100
accepted 5
trade 5 4 1 @ 101
trade 5 1 2 @ 100
= orders bids 1:100x3 2:100x6 3:100x7
= best bids 100
= resting 3
= last-accepted 5

# Cancelling the middle order moves the one behind it up.
> cancel 2
cancelled 2 6 requested
= orders bids 1:100x3 3:100x7

# A rejected order changes nothing, not even the last accepted id.
> new 5 buy limit 1 @ 100
rejected 5 id-not-increasing
= orders bids 1:100x3 3:100x7
= resting 2
= last-accepted 5

# A new order at the same price goes to the back of the queue.
> new 6 buy limit 2 @ 100
accepted 6
= orders bids 1:100x3 3:100x7 6:100x2

# A sell limit takes the whole queue in order and rests the rest, which becomes the best ask.
> new 7 sell limit 15 @ 100
accepted 7
trade 7 1 3 @ 100
trade 7 3 7 @ 100
trade 7 6 2 @ 100
= bids
= asks 100x3
= orders bids
= orders asks 7:100x3
= best bids none
= best asks 100
= resting 1
= last-accepted 7
